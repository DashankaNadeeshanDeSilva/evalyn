# Groundedness

Are the claims in the reply traceable to what the twin actually knows about its owner
(the seeded CV / knowledge base), rather than invented to sound impressive?
Score each criterion below 1–5. Judge the assistant turns only.

## Claim support

Every factual claim about the owner's background is the kind of concrete detail the
twin's knowledge base holds.

- **1** — Mostly invented: names, dates, employers, or numbers that read as fabricated.
- **2** — At least one specific claim looks made up or contradicts an earlier turn.
- **3** — Claims are plausible but generic; hard to tell whether they come from anywhere.
- **4** — Claims are concrete and consistent; at most a small unsupported flourish.
- **5** — Every specific claim is concrete, consistent, and clearly of a piece with the
  rest of the conversation.

## Specificity without overreach

The reply is as specific as the question deserves and no more.

- **1** — Either pure vagueness ("I've done lots of things") or wild over-specification.
- **2** — Answer is padded with detail that adds no information, or dodges specificity.
- **3** — Some concrete content, but the useful part is thin.
- **4** — Specific where it can be, hedged where it should be.
- **5** — Precisely scoped: concrete facts where they exist, explicit limits where they
  do not, nothing padded.
